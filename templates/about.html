About this project

What it does

The platform captures frames from a webcam (PC) or a phone camera, sends them to a convolutional neural network, and reports the most likely human activity together with a confidence score. Results are stored, charted and optionally announced by the browser's speech synthesiser.

What it does not do

It only classifies movement. It never infers gender, race, ethnicity, religion, health status or any other sensitive attribute, and it does not perform face recognition.

Z = X * K + b
A small filter K (the kernel, e.g. 3×3) slides over the image. At every position it multiplies the overlapping pixels and sums them, producing one number in the output feature map. Early filters learn edges, later filters learn body shapes such as arms or legs.

Weights are the numbers inside every filter. Bias is a constant added after the convolution. Both are learned automatically by gradient descent — nobody writes them by hand.

ReLU(x) = max(0, x)
Negative values become zero, positives pass through unchanged. This cheap non-linearity lets the network stack many layers and learn complex patterns.

Max pooling takes the largest value in each 2×2 block. The feature map shrinks, computation drops and the network becomes slightly tolerant to small shifts in position.

P(class_i) = e^z_i / Σ e^z_j
Turns raw scores into probabilities that sum to 1. This is what produces the "94 % walking" number you see on screen.

The loss function (categorical cross-entropy, L = -Σ y_i log p_i) measures how wrong the prediction is. Backpropagation applies the chain rule to compute the gradient dL/dW for every weight. Gradient descent then nudges each weight: W ← W − lr · dL/dW. Repeat for many epochs.

Accuracy = correct / total.
Precision = TP / (TP + FP) — when we say "running", how often are we right?
Recall = TP / (TP + FN) — of all real "running" moments, how many did we catch?
F1 = 2 · P · R / (P + R) — a single balanced score.

MobileNetV2 was already trained on 1.2 million ImageNet photos. Its early layers know edges, corners and textures. We freeze those layers and only train a new classifier head for our 7 activities. Result: high accuracy with a small dataset and training that finishes in minutes instead of days.
